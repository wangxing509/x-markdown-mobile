# Week 1 — The Terminology of AI

> Course: **AI for Everyone** by Andrew Ng (DeepLearning.AI / Coursera)
> Week: **1 — What Is AI** · Lesson: **The terminology of AI**
> Study notes (original summary, not verbatim transcript).

## Core Idea
This lesson builds a shared vocabulary so non-technical people can talk accurately with AI engineers and avoid misusing terms.

## Key Points
- **Machine Learning**: the subfield where computers learn from data; most of today's AI value. Its output is a **running AI system** that keeps performing an A→B task (e.g., a click-predictor operating 24/7).
- **Data Science**: related but distinct. ML builds systems that learn A→B; data science extracts **insights** from data to inform decisions. Its output is typically a **report / slide deck** (e.g., "renovated homes carry a 15% premium"). The two are often confused.
- **Deep Learning** (a.k.a. neural networks): a powerful subfield of ML that has driven recent breakthroughs, especially for complex tasks like image/audio/language. The name "deep learning" is largely a rebranding of neural networks.
- **The hierarchy**: **Deep learning ⊂ Machine learning ⊂ AI**. Not all AI is ML, and not all ML is deep learning. Data science sits alongside these (its exact relation to "AI" is debated).
- **Practical takeaway**: The best AI projects start by identifying a **specific A→B task**, then asking which tool (ML, data science, etc.) fits — not by chasing buzzwords.
- **Why terminology matters**: Business leaders who use terms loosely (e.g., "we'll just add AI") struggle to scope projects; precise framing leads to realistic expectations.

## Takeaways
- Distinguish **ML (builds models)** from **data science (builds insights)**.
- Learn the hierarchy: **Deep learning → Machine learning → AI**.
- Use the A→B task framing to ground discussions with technical teams.
