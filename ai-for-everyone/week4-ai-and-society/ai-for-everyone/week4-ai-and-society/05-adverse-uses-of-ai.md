# Week 4 — Adverse Uses of AI

> Course: **AI for Everyone** by Andrew Ng (DeepLearning.AI / Coursera)
> Week: **4 — AI & Society** · Lesson: **Adverse uses of AI**
> Study notes (original summary, not verbatim transcript).

## Core Idea
Beyond accidental harm, AI can be **deliberately misused** to cause damage — and this is a distinct concern from adversarial attacks on models.

## Key Points
- **What "adverse uses" means**: applying AI intentionally to produce harm — for example creating misleading content, enabling fraud, or amplifying disinformation.
- **Key example — deepfakes**:
  - Generative models can create realistic fake **images, audio, and video** of real people saying or doing things they never did.
  - Risks: reputation damage, fraud (fake voice to authorize transactions), political disinformation, and erosion of trust in real media.
- **Other adverse uses**:
  - **Fake text/comments**: AI-generated comments used to manipulate public opinion.
  - **Government surveillance**: some governments using AI for oppressive surveillance of their populations.
  - Generating spam/phishing at scale, automated harassment, and tools that amplify manipulation.
- **Why it's hard to stop**: the same generative capabilities that create legitimate content can be turned to harmful ends; detecting fakes is an arms race.
- **Response**: a combination of **detection tools, policy/regulation, platform responsibility, and public awareness** ("don't believe everything you see").
- **Leadership angle**: organizations should consider the adverse-use risks of the AI they build or deploy and put guardrails in place.

## Takeaways
- Adverse use = deliberately weaponizing AI (e.g., deepfakes), distinct from adversarial input attacks.
- Realistic AI security includes **detection, policy, and education**.
- Responsible AI means anticipating misuse, not just technical accuracy.
