# Week 2 — Working with an AI Team

> Course: **AI for Everyone** by Andrew Ng (DeepLearning.AI / Coursera)
> Week: **2 — Building AI Projects** · Lesson: **Working with an AI team**
> Study notes (original summary, not verbatim transcript).

## Core Idea
Non-technical stakeholders work best with AI teams when they understand the team's roles, communicate expectations clearly, and let the team own the technical solution.

## Key Points
- **Typical AI team roles**:
  - **Software engineer** — builds the broader product the AI sits in.
  - **Machine learning engineer** — builds and deploys the learning system.
  - **Machine learning researcher** — develops new model approaches (more common on hard, novel problems).
  - **Applied ML scientist** — adapts research into practical solutions for a specific domain.
  - **Data scientist** — analyzes data and extracts insights.
  - **Data engineer** — sets up the data pipelines and infrastructure.
  - **AI product manager** — translates business needs into specs and priorities.
- **Effective collaboration tips**:
  - **Be specific about the business goal**, not the technical approach — "reduce churn by X%" rather than "use a neural network."
  - **Share your domain knowledge**; a great AI team + a great domain expert outperforms either alone.
  - **Set expectations on data**: the team can't conjure good results from poor/missing data.
  - **Expect iteration**: ask for milestones and progress, but understand accuracy improves over rounds.
  - **A leader's role**: specify the project **and the success metric** without writing code — that single discipline drives good outcomes.
- **Avoid the "black box" trap**: insist on understandable, testable criteria for success, even if the internal model is complex.

## Takeaways
- Understand the roles so you can bring the right person to the table.
- Communicate business outcomes; let the team own technical implementation.
- Combine domain knowledge with technical talent for best results.
