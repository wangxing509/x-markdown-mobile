# Week 2 — Workflow of a Data Science Project

> Course: **AI for Everyone** by Andrew Ng (DeepLearning.AI / Coursera)
> Week: **2 — Building AI Projects** · Lesson: **Workflow of a data science project**
> Study notes (original summary, not verbatim transcript).

## Core Idea
Data science is about producing **insights** (and recommendations) from data to support decisions — a different deliverable from the model built in an ML project.

## Key Points
- **Data science workflow**:
  1. **Collect data** relevant to the business question.
  2. **Analyze** the data to find patterns, correlations, and trends.
  3. **Propose hypotheses** and test them; iterate on what to look at.
  4. **Deliver insights/decisions** — often a report, dashboard, or recommendation to leadership.
- **Deliverable = insight, not software**: success is measured by whether the insight improves a decision, not by a model's accuracy.
- **Difference from ML project**: ML builds a system that keeps predicting A→B in production; data science produces a **one-time (or periodic) analysis** that changes a decision.
- **In practice**: the two overlap — data scientists often build small models to generate insights, and ML teams use data-science-style analysis to understand problems.

## Takeaways
- Frame a data science engagement as "we want an answer to a question," not "we want a product."
- Success metric: did the analysis inform a better decision?
- Keep the ML vs. data-science deliverable distinction clear when scoping work.
